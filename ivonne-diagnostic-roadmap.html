05 · Distribution

Her Facebook Group first, then Instagram and YouTube

She has a 3,000-member Facebook Group, which in 2026 is a far more valuable asset than the follower counts suggest. Group posts reach 20 to 50% of members while Page posts reach roughly 1.4%. Her Group is the single strongest distribution channel she owns, and it is also the right place for a controlled first launch.

Where the funnel actually breaks

Widest at the top, narrowest at the bottom. The highlighted stage is the one costing the most right now.

Nearly everyone over-produces for the first two rows and builds nothing for the middle. Those middle rows are where buying decisions are actually made.

How this platform actually works right now

1 A 3,000-member Group can reach 600 to 1,500 people per post organically

The same audience on a Page would see a fraction of that. Her Group is worth more than a much larger Page following, and should carry the launch.

2 Launch small and capped on purpose

Selling out a small controlled group builds more demand than half-filling a large one, and it stress-tests the fulfilment system before wider traffic arrives.

3 Groups convert dramatically better than cold audiences

Well-run communities convert at many multiples of cold traffic, which is exactly why the first launch belongs there and not on IG or YouTube.

4 Sends are the ranking signal on Instagram when she expands

The Living Book concept is inherently shareable between friends, which is the behaviour Instagram now rewards most.

5 Do not launch the Group with heavy sales content

Communities that open with hard selling stall early and struggle to recover trust. Value first, offer second, even in her own group.

How the content engine fits in

The marketing officer is where Jarvis does the work. Voice profile and positioning go in once, then every draft comes out already sounding like Ivonne rather than needing a rewrite. The job stops being writing from scratch and becomes editing something that is already close.

The rule that makes this work: talk first, judge later. Speak the raw idea, let the draft come back, then edit. Doing all three at once is what makes content feel slow.

What to hand over, and what never to

Hand over the first draft, the research, the repurposing, and the follow-up sequences. Those are volume tasks and the quality bar is reachable.

Keep the judgment: which client to take, what to charge, what to say when something goes wrong, and any opinion with Ivonne's name on it. The bar for letting a system run unsupervised is simple. Out of fifty drafts, would forty-eight go out unedited? Until then, review every one.

07 · Execution

The 60-day roadmap

Four phases, same framework as the rest of the cohort: Foundation, Build, Launch and Test, Scale. Sequenced around Ivonne's specific bottleneck, not a generic order.

1. Foundation
2. Build
3. Launch & Test
4. Scale
The operating principle behind this sequence

A funnel has four jobs: trigger attention, capture it, nurture it, then ask. Skipping capture means everyone reached is rented, not owned. That's the one piece to build before any wider launch.

PHASE 1 Days 1–15 · Foundation

  • Set up GoHighLevel. CRM, community, funnels, and automation in one place
  • Build the order/fulfillment flow for the Living Book before launch: purchase → chapter delivery → card claim → support routing
  • Draft a support FAQ/script for the most likely confusion points, including 'what if someone doesn't want it after downloading'
Proves it worked: End-to-end fulfillment flow built and tested before launch day.

PHASE 2 Days 16–30 · Build

  • Launch the Living Book to the existing 3,000-person Facebook group first. A controlled test before wider reach
  • Turn on AI-assisted content production only once the system is proven, not before
  • Set up automated message triage so incoming messages get routed without her personally reading every one
Proves it worked: First launch cohort processed without repeating the 2020 overwhelm.

PHASE 3 Days 31–45 · Launch & Test

  • Expand to IG/YouTube audiences only after the smaller test held
  • Run the first in-person event tied to the card-game mechanic, using the systems built to handle logistics
Proves it worked: First event run with systemized logistics, not a manual scramble.

PHASE 4 Days 46–60 · Scale

  • Review what broke at wider scale and fix before any paid promotion or virality push
  • Begin early Uber of Work scoping. Deliberately sequenced last, since it's the far bigger build
Proves it worked: Systems validated at scale before the next venture starts.
